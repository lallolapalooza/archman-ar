NAME

tplqt2

computes a block reflector of an upper trapezoidal matrix

SYNOPSIS

SUBROUTINE ZTPLQT2( M, N, L, A, LDA, B, LDB, V, LDV, INFO )
    INTEGER           M, N, L, LDA, LDB, LDV, INFO
    COMPLEX*16        A( LDA, * ), B( LDB, * ), V( LDV, * )

Detailed Description

This routine is the entry point to the recursive blocked routines that compute the ZTPQRT2 factorization.

The algorithm used is described in

   T. Davis, P. Boon, and W. Van Assche, "An Lq&lt;R&gt;Q-factorization
   for the Rank-Revealing QR factorization", ACM Trans. Math.
   Software, 32(4):525-541, 2006.

The routines compute the ZTPQRT2 factorization of a real block matrix:

$$ C = \begin{bmatrix} A & B \end{bmatrix} $$

where A is an M-by-M upper triangular matrix and B is an M-by-N upper trapezoidal matrix.

The ZTPQRT2 factorization is:

$$ C = \begin{bmatrix} R_{11} & R_{12} & 0 \\ 0 & R_{22} & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} Q_1^H \\ Q_2^H \end{bmatrix} $$

where

  • $R_{11}$ is M-by-M upper triangular, and $R_{22}$ is L-by-L upper triangular.

  • $$ Q = \begin{bmatrix} Q_1 \\ Q_2 \end{bmatrix} $$

is an (M+N)-by-(M+N) unitary matrix, with $Q_1$ M-by-(M+N) and $Q_2$ N-by-(M+N).

The factorization is computed by the recursive blocked routine ZTPQRT.

This routine is the entry point to the recursive blocked routines that compute the ZTPQRT2 factorization.

The input matrix C is a M-by-(M+N) matrix

$$ C = \begin{bmatrix} A & B \end{bmatrix} $$

where A is an lower triangular M-by-M matrix, and B is M-by-N pentagonal matrix consisting of a M-by-(N-L) rectangular matrix B1 left of a M-by-L upper trapezoidal matrix B2:

$$ B = \begin{bmatrix} B1 & B2 \end{bmatrix} $$

The lower trapezoidal matrix B2 consists of the first L columns of a N-by-N lower triangular matrix, where 0 <= L < MIN(M,N). If L=0, B is rectangular M-by-N; if M=L=N, B is lower triangular.

The matrix W stores the elementary reflectors H(i) in the i-th row above the diagonal (of A) in the M-by-(M+N) input matrix C

$$ C = \begin{bmatrix} A & B \end{bmatrix} $$

so that W can be represented as

$$ W = \begin{bmatrix} I & V \end{bmatrix} $$

where I is identity, M-by-M, and V is M-by-N, same form as B.

Thus, all of information needed for W is contained on exit in B, which we call V above. Note that V has the same form as B; that is,

$$ W = \begin{bmatrix} V1 & V2 \end{bmatrix} $$

The rows of V represent the vectors which define the H(i)'s. The (M+N)-by-(M+N) block reflector H is then given by

$$ H = I - W^H T W $$

where $W^H$ is the conjugate transpose of W and T is the upper triangular factor of the block reflector.
